Is the Bible Trustworthy?

Reading Library

The Bible makes enormous claims.

It tells us who God is, why humanity is broken, what sin has done, what happens after death, and how sinners can be reconciled to their Creator through Jesus Christ.

Those claims deserve an honest question:

Can the Bible be trusted?

The answer is yes.

The Bible is not a damaged religious relic, a collection of legends, or a message lost through centuries of copying. It is the Word of God, given through human authors, preserved through history, and faithfully delivered to us as God intended.

God Is the Author of Scripture

The Bible was written through real people, in real places, during real periods of history. Its writers had different backgrounds, personalities, experiences, and styles.

Yet behind them stood one ultimate Author.

“All Scripture is given by inspiration of God.”

2 Timothy 3:16, NKJV

The word “inspiration” does not mean that the biblical writers merely felt religious or creative. Scripture came from God.

“Holy men of God spoke as they were moved by the Holy Spirit.”

2 Peter 1:21, NKJV

The Holy Spirit did not erase the personalities of the writers. He worked through them so that what they wrote was truly their writing and, at the same time, truly the Word of God.

Ultimately, the Holy Spirit gets all authority for the Bible we have today.

He inspired it.

He preserved it.

He guided its recognition among God’s people.

The Bible is as God intended, period.

The Bible Has Been Faithfully Preserved

The Scriptures were copied and carried across centuries, languages, nations, persecution, war, and cultural change.

Yet their message has not been lost.

God did not inspire His Word and then abandon it to destruction or confusion.

“Heaven and earth will pass away, but My words will by no means pass away.”

Matthew 24:35, NKJV

“The grass withers, the flower fades,
But the word of our God stands forever.”

Isaiah 40:8, NKJV

The Bible available to us today faithfully communicates what God gave.

There are differences in wording among trustworthy translations because languages do not always match one another word for word. Translators must make careful decisions about grammar, sentence structure, emphasis, and how best to express the original meaning clearly.

Those differences do not mean we have different Bibles.

They mean faithful translators are working to communicate the same God-given message accurately and understandably.

Mainstream translations such as the NKJV, ESV, NRSV, and others reliably give readers the Word of God.

The Bible Does Not Contain True Errors or Contradictions

The Bible is truthful because God is truthful.

“God is not a man, that He should lie.”

Numbers 23:19, NKJV

“Your word is truth.”

John 17:17, NKJV

Some passages require careful reading. Some accounts emphasize different details. Some statements must be understood according to their historical setting, literary form, language, and immediate context.

But difficulty is not the same as error.

Different perspectives are not contradictions.

The Bible does not affirm and deny the same truth in the same sense.

It speaks truthfully in everything it teaches.

The Bible Is Rooted in Real History

The Bible does not begin with “once upon a time.”

It speaks of real nations, rulers, cities, families, wars, journeys, customs, and places.

Its message unfolds within history.

Abraham traveled.

Israel became a nation.

David ruled.

Jerusalem stood.

Babylon conquered.

Jesus was born under Roman rule.

He taught publicly, was crucified under Pontius Pilate, died, was buried, and rose from the dead.

Christian faith is not built merely on private feelings or spiritual ideas. It rests on what God has done in the real world.

Luke explained that he had carefully investigated the events he recorded so that his reader could know their certainty (Luke 1:1–4).

“For we did not follow cunningly devised fables.”

2 Peter 1:16, NKJV

The Bible invites examination because its claims are tied to history.

The Bible Tells One Unified Story

The Bible contains many books, yet together they tell one great story.

God created the world good.

Humanity rebelled.

Sin brought guilt, separation, suffering, and death.

God promised redemption.

He formed a covenant people.

He revealed His law, holiness, justice, and mercy.

He promised a Savior.

Jesus Christ came.

He died for sinners.

He rose from the grave.

He will return, judge evil, and restore all things.

The Bible’s central message is not scattered or confused.

It leads to Jesus Christ.

“And beginning at Moses and all the Prophets, He expounded to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning Himself.”

Luke 24:27, NKJV

The Bible is not merely a collection of religious advice.

It is the unfolding account of God’s work to redeem sinners through His Son.

The Bible Tells the Truth About Humanity

The Bible does not flatter us.

It does not pretend that people are basically righteous and merely need better information.

“All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.”

Romans 3:23, NKJV

It exposes pride, greed, lust, hatred, hypocrisy, violence, self-deception, and rebellion.

It also tells the truth about its own human figures.

Noah failed.

Abraham feared.

Jacob deceived.

Moses disobeyed.

David sinned terribly.

Peter denied Jesus.

The disciples argued, doubted, and fled.

A book invented merely to honor its heroes would likely hide their failures.

The Bible does not.

It presents human beings honestly because its purpose is not to glorify people. Its purpose is to reveal God.

Jesus Trusted Scripture Completely

Jesus treated Scripture as the authoritative Word of God.

He quoted it when tempted.

He used it when teaching.

He appealed to it in disputes.

“The Scripture cannot be broken.”

John 10:35, NKJV

“It is written.”

Matthew 4:4, NKJV

Jesus did not treat Scripture as a mixture of truth and error.

He trusted it, obeyed it, fulfilled it, and taught others to receive it as God’s Word.

The most important question, then, is not merely whether we approve of the Bible.

It is whether we trust Jesus Christ.

If Jesus is who He claimed to be, His view of Scripture carries absolute authority.

The Bible Changes Lives Because God Speaks Through It

The power of the Bible does not come merely from beautiful sentences or ancient wisdom.

God uses His Word to expose sin, awaken faith, correct error, comfort the grieving, strengthen the weak, and lead people to salvation.

“For the word of God is living and powerful, and sharper than any two-edged sword.”

Hebrews 4:12, NKJV

“Faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.”

Romans 10:17, NKJV

People do not merely read the Bible.

Through it, they encounter the truth of God.

Its message has endured because God continues to use it.

The Bible’s Central Message Is Jesus Christ

The Bible tells us that God is holy and that we are sinners.

Our sin separates us from Him.

Good deeds cannot erase guilt.

Religion cannot make us righteous.

We cannot save ourselves.

But God did not leave us without hope.

Jesus Christ, the Son of God, came into the world. He lived without sin. He died on the cross in the place of sinners. He bore the judgment our sin deserves. On the third day, He rose from the dead.

“Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, and that He was buried, and that He rose again the third day according to the Scriptures.”

1 Corinthians 15:3–4, NKJV

The Bible was not given merely to make us informed.

It was given to make us wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus (2 Timothy 3:15).

Will You Trust What God Has Said?

You may still have questions.

You may need time to read, study, and examine the evidence.

That is not wrong.

But the Bible does more than ask for intellectual respect. It brings you face to face with God’s truth.

It says that you were created by Him.

It says that you have sinned against Him.

It says that judgment is real.

It says that Jesus Christ died and rose again.

It says that forgiveness and eternal life are offered to everyone who repents and trusts Him.

“He who hears My word and believes in Him who sent Me has everlasting life, and shall not come into judgment, but has passed from death into life.”

John 5:24, NKJV

The Bible is trustworthy because the God who gave it is trustworthy.

Its message has not been lost.

Its truth has not expired.

Its authority has not weakened.

The Bible is as God intended.

And its central invitation remains open:

Turn from sin.

Come to Jesus Christ.

Trust Him.

Receive forgiveness.

Receive eternal life.

The question is no longer only, “Can I trust the Bible?”

The deeper question is:

Will I trust the Savior revealed in its pages?

If you are ready to trust Jesus Christ, ask Solomon 2.0, “How can I be saved?”

Wisdom. Truth. Hope in Jesus Christ.
