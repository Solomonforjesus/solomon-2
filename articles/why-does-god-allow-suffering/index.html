Why Does God Allow Suffering?

Reading Library

Suffering is easier to discuss from a distance.

It is different when the hospital room is yours, the grave belongs to someone you love, the betrayal has your name attached to it, or the prayer you have repeated for months still seems unanswered.

At that point, “Why does God allow suffering?” is no longer merely a philosophical question. It becomes personal.

Why did God allow this?

Does He see me?

Does He care?

Can I still trust Him?

The Bible does not dismiss those questions. It does not command hurting people to pretend that pain is painless. It speaks honestly about grief, disease, injustice, death, fear, and loss. It also tells us truths about God that remain true even when life becomes difficult to understand.

God Is Sovereign Over All, Including Suffering

Nothing takes God by surprise.

He never learns bad news. He is never caught unprepared. No disaster forces Him to abandon His purposes or invent a new plan.

“I form the light and create darkness,
I make peace and create calamity;
I, the Lord, do all these things.”

Isaiah 45:7, NKJV

This verse does not teach that God commits moral evil. Scripture is clear that God is holy, righteous, and good. It teaches that He remains sovereign over nations, judgment, peace, prosperity, and calamity. Nothing exists outside His authority.

That truth may raise another difficult question: If God is sovereign, why does He permit suffering at all?

The Bible does not give us a complete explanation for every individual tragedy. It does tell us that suffering does not mean God has lost control.

We may be confused. God is not.

We may be frightened. God is not.

We may not see what comes next. God already does.

God’s Ways Are Higher Than Ours

“For My thoughts are not your thoughts,
Nor are your ways My ways,” says the Lord.
“For as the heavens are higher than the earth,
So are My ways higher than your ways,
And My thoughts than your thoughts.”

Isaiah 55:8–9, NKJV

Human understanding is limited. We see one moment, one life, and one small portion of history. God sees the whole.

That does not mean we should use these verses to silence someone who is grieving. “God’s ways are higher” should never become a cold answer thrown at a broken heart.

It should produce humility.

Faith does not require us to claim that we understand what God has not explained. Sometimes the honest answer is:

I do not know why God allowed this particular suffering.

The book of Job teaches this powerfully. Job lost his children, wealth, health, and position. His friends confidently insisted that his suffering must have been caused by some hidden personal sin. They were wrong.

Job never received a detailed explanation for everything that had happened to him. Instead, he encountered the greatness, wisdom, and authority of God.

There are times when God reveals His purposes. There are also times when He asks us to trust His character while the reason remains hidden.

We Live in a Fallen World

The Bible begins with a world that God called “very good” (Genesis 1:31).

Human beings were created to know God, love Him, obey Him, and live under His good authority. But humanity rebelled. Sin entered the world, and with sin came death, separation, corruption, and disorder.

“Therefore, just as through one man sin entered the world, and death through sin, and thus death spread to all men, because all sinned.”

Romans 5:12, NKJV

The effects of the Fall reach beyond individual bad choices. Human bodies age and become ill. Relationships fracture. Nations wage war. People exploit one another. Creation itself is marked by decay and suffering.

“For we know that the whole creation groans and labors with birth pangs together until now.”

Romans 8:22, NKJV

This means suffering is not always a direct punishment aimed at one particular person.

Jesus made that clear.

When His disciples met a man who had been blind from birth, they asked whether the man or his parents had sinned and caused his condition. Jesus rejected both proposed explanations and said that the works of God would be revealed in him (John 9:1–3).

When people told Jesus about the Galileans whom Pilate had killed, He rejected the idea that they were worse sinners than other Galileans. Jesus then pointed to a separate tragedy—the eighteen people killed when the tower of Siloam fell—and said they were not worse offenders than the other people living in Jerusalem. In both cases, He warned His hearers of their own need to repent (Luke 13:1–5).

We should therefore never look at someone’s cancer, disability, poverty, bereavement, accident, or tragedy and pretend that we know what private sin caused it.

Sometimes people suffer because of their own choices.

Sometimes they suffer because of another person’s cruelty, negligence, violence, or betrayal.

Sometimes they suffer through disease, aging, accidents, or natural disasters.

Sometimes they suffer for doing what is right.

Sometimes the immediate reason remains unknown.

But all suffering occurs within a world that is no longer as God originally made it.

Human Sin Causes Enormous Suffering

Much of the world’s suffering comes directly from human rebellion against God.

War, murder, abuse, exploitation, abandonment, corruption, oppression, adultery, hatred, and greed are not abstract ideas. They leave real victims behind.

God has given human beings genuine moral responsibility. Love, loyalty, courage, obedience, generosity, and sacrifice have meaning because our choices are real. But real choices can also be used for terrible evil.

God’s temporary permission of human evil is not His approval of it.

He condemns injustice. He commands people to protect the vulnerable. He establishes moral accountability. He warns that every person will answer to Him.

“For God will bring every work into judgment,
Including every secret thing,
Whether good or evil.”

Ecclesiastes 12:14, NKJV

No hidden cruelty will remain hidden forever.

No unpunished crime has escaped God’s notice.

No victim has been forgotten.

Earthly justice may fail. God’s final justice will not.

God’s Mercy Has Not Ended

Suffering reveals the brokenness of the world, but it does not mean God’s mercy has disappeared.

Jeremiah wrote during a period of terrible national destruction:

“Through the Lord’s mercies we are not consumed,
Because His compassions fail not.
They are new every morning;
Great is Your faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23, NKJV

These words were not written by someone whose life was easy. They came from a man who had witnessed devastation and grief.

Even in a fallen world, God continues to show mercy.

Every breath is mercy.

Every act of genuine love reflects His goodness.

Every meal, sunrise, friendship, answered prayer, moment of comfort, and opportunity to repent is undeserved kindness.

This does not make suffering unimportant. It reminds us that suffering is not the only reality present in the world. God has not withdrawn all goodness from His creation.

More importantly, He has not withheld the greatest expression of His mercy: His Son.

Suffering Can Be a Wake-Up Call

Suffering often destroys the illusion that we are self-sufficient.

When health fails, plans collapse, or death comes near, we are reminded that we are not in control. We are mortal. We need help beyond ourselves.

“In my distress I cried to the Lord, And He heard me.”

Psalm 120:1, NKJV

God can use suffering to turn a person’s attention back toward Him.

It may expose sin we have ignored.

It may reveal relationships that need repair.

It may break pride.

It may deepen compassion.

It may teach perseverance.

It may awaken someone who has lived for years as though God did not exist.

But we must be careful here. We should not presume to know that every tragedy was sent to teach a particular person a particular lesson. Nor should we tell a suffering person that their pain exists only because they failed to listen to God.

Scripture allows us to say that God can use suffering.

It does not give us permission to pretend that we understand His exact purpose in every case.

God Can Bring Good from Evil Without Calling Evil Good

“And we know that all things work together for good to those who love God, to those who are the called according to His purpose.”

Romans 8:28, NKJV

The verse does not say that everything that happens is good.

Cancer is not good.

Abuse is not good.

Murder is not good.

Betrayal is not good.

Death is an enemy.

But God is so wise and powerful that evil cannot prevent Him from accomplishing His purposes.

Joseph was betrayed by his brothers, sold into slavery, falsely accused, and imprisoned. Years later, he told them:

“But as for you, you meant evil against me; but God meant it for good.”

Genesis 50:20, NKJV

Joseph did not call their evil good. He said God overruled their evil intentions and used the outcome to preserve many lives.

God can bring courage from hardship, compassion from grief, ministry from wounds, repentance from failure, and faith from desperation.

Still, not every painful event will make complete sense in this lifetime. Some wounds remain wounds. Some questions remain unanswered.

The Christian hope is not that every tragedy will eventually feel acceptable.

It is that evil will not have the final word.

The Bible Gives Us Permission to Grieve

Biblical faith is not emotional denial.

Job grieved.

Jeremiah wept.

David cried out in anguish.

Habakkuk asked why God seemed silent.

Jesus wept at the grave of Lazarus.

“How long, O Lord? Will You forget me forever?”

Psalm 13:1, NKJV

These are not the words of unbelief pretending to be faith. They are the words of faith speaking honestly to God.

You may grieve before Him.

You may tell Him that you are angry, confused, frightened, or exhausted.

You do not need to hide your pain behind religious language.

God already knows what is in your heart.

Honest grief is not the opposite of faith. Sometimes it is faith crying out to the only One who can truly hear.

God Did Not Remain Distant from Human Suffering

Christianity’s deepest answer to suffering is not merely an explanation.

It is Jesus Christ.

God the Son entered the world He had made. He took on human flesh. He experienced hunger, exhaustion, grief, rejection, betrayal, injustice, humiliation, physical agony, and death.

“A Man of sorrows and acquainted with grief.”

Isaiah 53:3, NKJV

Jesus wept with grieving people.

He touched the diseased.

He welcomed the rejected.

He defended the vulnerable.

He showed compassion to the guilty.

And at the cross, He willingly bore the judgment that sinners deserve.

“But He was wounded for our transgressions,
He was bruised for our iniquities;
The chastisement for our peace was upon Him,
And by His stripes we are healed.”

Isaiah 53:5, NKJV

At the cross, God did not explain suffering from a safe distance.

He entered it.

Jesus was not helplessly trapped. He gave Himself willingly.

He suffered for our sins so that those who trust Him could be forgiven, reconciled to God, and given eternal life.

The cross shows us that God does not take evil lightly. Sin is so serious that the Son of God bore its judgment.

The cross also shows us that God’s love is not theoretical. Jesus suffered to rescue people who had rebelled against Him.

The Resurrection Changes the Ending

Jesus truly died.

But death did not defeat Him.

On the third day, He rose bodily from the grave. His resurrection is God’s declaration that sin, death, and evil will not prevail.

“I am the resurrection and the life. He who believes in Me, though he may die, he shall live.”

John 11:25, NKJV

Christianity does not promise that believers will escape all suffering in this life.

Jesus told His followers that they would face trouble.

But He also promised that suffering is temporary and His victory is eternal.

“For I consider that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which shall be revealed in us.”

Romans 8:18, NKJV

For those who belong to Christ, this world is not the final home.

There is a day coming when God will judge evil, raise the dead, restore creation, and dwell with His people.

“And God will wipe away every tear from their eyes; there shall be no more death, nor sorrow, nor crying. There shall be no more pain.”

Revelation 21:4, NKJV

This is not wishful thinking. It is the promised future secured by the resurrection of Jesus Christ.

Can You Trust God While You Are Suffering?

You may not know why God allowed the pain you are carrying.

You may never receive a full explanation in this life.

But you can know what God has revealed about Himself.

He is sovereign.

He is holy.

He is just.

He is merciful.

He sees the brokenhearted.

He judges evil.

He entered human suffering in Jesus Christ.

He died for sinners.

He rose from the dead.

He promises to make all things right.

Your pain does not prove that God has abandoned you.

Your questions do not frighten Him.

Your grief does not place you beyond His compassion.

“The Lord is near to those who have a broken heart,
And saves such as have a contrite spirit.”

Psalm 34:18, NKJV

You may need help from people as well. Seeking medical treatment, counseling, pastoral care, legal protection, or emergency assistance is not a failure of faith. Forgiveness does not require you to remain in danger or permit continuing abuse. God often provides help through other people.

Do not carry suffering alone.

Jesus Invites You to Come

The greatest need of every human being is reconciliation with God.

Our suffering is real, but so is our sin. We have all broken God’s commands, rejected His authority, and fallen short of His glory.

We cannot save ourselves through good deeds, religion, or sincerity.

But Jesus has done what we could never do.

He lived without sin.

He died in the place of sinners.

He rose from the dead.

He now offers forgiveness and eternal life to everyone who turns from sin and trusts Him.

“Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28, NKJV

He does not promise that every earthly burden will disappear immediately.

He promises something greater: His presence, forgiveness, peace with God, strength to endure, and eternal life.

You are free to reject Him.

You are also free to come.

He will not force your trust. Love does not compel a false response. But He genuinely invites you.

You may come honestly, even if your faith feels small.

You may say:

“Lord Jesus, I do not understand everything I have suffered. I confess that I am a sinner and cannot save myself. I believe You died for my sins and rose from the dead. Please forgive me, receive me, and teach me to trust You. I place my life in Your hands. Amen.”

The prayer itself does not save you. Jesus saves. The prayer is simply one way of expressing sincere repentance and faith in Him.

We may not know why God permitted every sorrow.

But we know that He is not indifferent.

He sees.

He cares.

He judges rightly.

He draws near to the brokenhearted.

And in Jesus Christ, He has opened the way from suffering and death into forgiveness, resurrection, and everlasting life.

The wounds of this world are real.

But they are not eternal.

Jesus Christ is.

If you are ready to turn to Jesus Christ, ask Solomon 2.0, “How can I be saved?”

Wisdom. Truth. Hope in Jesus Christ.
