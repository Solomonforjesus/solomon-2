What Is the Gospel?

Reading Library

The Gospel is often reduced to a slogan because its words are familiar. But familiar words can conceal an earthshaking truth.

The Gospel is not advice about how people might climb their way to God. It is the announcement that God came down to rescue people who could not save themselves.

Humanity’s separation from God is real. Our attempts to repair it are powerless. God Himself has crossed the distance in Jesus Christ.

That is the Gospel.

Something Has Gone Terribly Wrong

We do not need to be theologians to recognize that the world is not as it should be.

We see cruelty, betrayal, injustice, disease, grief, loneliness, aging, and death. We watch nations rage against nations. Families fracture. People wound one another, sometimes deliberately and sometimes through selfishness, carelessness, or pride.

We feel the fracture within ourselves as well.

We know what it is to regret words we cannot retrieve. We make promises and break them. We recognize what is right, yet still choose what is wrong. Even our finest moments are mixed with motives we would rather not examine too closely.

Something has gone wrong with the world, and something has gone wrong within us.

The Bible calls that condition sin.

Sin is more than violating a list of religious rules. It is humanity turning away from its Creator and attempting to live independently of Him. It is the creature saying to the Creator, in countless ways, “I will decide for myself what is good, what is true, and how my life should be lived.”

Sin separates us from the holy God who gave us life.

“For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.”

Romans 3:23, NKJV

We Are Not Only Victims

It is easier to see the evil done to us than the evil that passes through us.

We may blame our upbringing, circumstances, culture, temptations, or the failures of others. Those things can wound us deeply and shape our lives powerfully. But none of them fully removes our responsibility before God.

Human beings are victims of a broken world, but we are also participants in its brokenness.

The darkness is not found only in dictators, criminals, or distant societies. It appears in ordinary hearts through envy, dishonesty, lust, hatred, greed, arrogance, bitterness, and indifference.

The Gospel begins with honesty.

We cannot understand the greatness of rescue until we understand the reality of the danger.

We Cannot Repair the Separation

Most people try, in one way or another, to make themselves acceptable.

Some attempt it through religion. Others through charity, morality, success, knowledge, political causes, personal discipline, or simply trying to be better than the people around them.

Good works matter. Kindness matters. Justice matters. Obedience matters. But none of these can erase guilt or make a sinful person holy before God.

A person may polish the outside of life while remaining separated from God within.

We cannot undo yesterday’s sin by behaving better tomorrow. We cannot repay an infinite moral debt with a collection of finite good deeds. We cannot climb high enough, work hard enough, become religious enough, or reinvent ourselves thoroughly enough to reach God.

The distance is not measured in miles.

It is moral and spiritual.

And we are powerless to cross it.

God Crossed the Distance

This is where the Gospel becomes good news.

God did not abandon humanity to its rebellion. He did not merely send another teacher, philosopher, prophet, or moral reformer. He came to us in Jesus Christ.

The eternal Son of God entered the human condition. Jesus lived among the people He had created. He experienced hunger, exhaustion, rejection, sorrow, temptation, suffering, and death—yet He remained without sin.

He did what we could not do.

Then He willingly went to the cross.

The cross was not an unfortunate ending to a promising life. It was the purpose for which He came. Jesus bore the judgment sinners deserved. He stood in the place of the guilty and gave His life as the sacrifice for sin.

God’s justice was not ignored. His holiness was not compromised. Sin was judged, and mercy was offered.

“But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8, NKJV

The Gospel is not the story of humanity finally finding a path upward.

It is the story of God coming down.

He Was Doing This for You

It is easy to look at the cross as an event from long ago—something that happened to Jesus in another place, among another people, in another time.

But the cross was not distant from you.

Imagine Jesus raising His head beneath the crushing weight of His suffering. Through the blood, the pain, and the anguish, His piercing eyes look beyond the soldiers, beyond the mocking crowd, and beyond Jerusalem.

He peers through all of history—past, present, and future.

His eyes focus upon every person who has ever lived and every person who ever will.

And then they focus upon you.

It is as though Jesus is saying:

“I AM doing this for you! Please do not let My pain and anguish go unheeded. Love Me. Trust Me. Trust Me alone!”

The cross was not an impersonal payment made for a faceless mass of humanity. Jesus knew exactly whom He was dying to save.

He saw every failure.

Every betrayal.

Every hidden sin.

Every broken promise.

Every act of selfishness, rebellion, cruelty, and unbelief.

He saw the worst in us—and He continued toward the cross.

The nails were real.

The wounds were real.

The humiliation was real.

The anguish was real.

And His love was real.

Then, as Jesus hung upon the cross, the world went dark.

The sinless Son of God was carrying the accumulated guilt of humanity. Every sin of the past, every sin being committed at that very moment, and every sin that would ever be committed was placed upon Him.

Father God accepted the offering of Jesus Christ as enough to pay for all sin for all time.

But God is holy. He cannot dwell in the presence of sin. And as Jesus bore the full weight of humanity’s sin, the Father had to turn His back upon His Son.

For the first time in all eternity, Jesus was literally alone.

The One who had always known the perfect presence of His Father could no longer sense Him at His side.

Out of that unbearable darkness, Jesus cried:

“My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?”

Matthew 27:46, NKJV

Consider how terrifying that must have been for our King.

The physical torture was horrific. His body had been beaten, torn, pierced, and nailed to wood. Breathing required agony. The people He came to save mocked Him as He died.

But now there was something even more terrible.

His Father had turned away.

Jesus stood alone beneath the entire judgment of God against sin.

He could have ended it.

He had already said that He could call upon His Father and more than twelve legions of angels would come to Him. Heaven’s armies stood ready. At one word from their King, they could have descended upon those who mocked, beat, and crucified Him.

And who could have blamed Him?

Who could have blamed Jesus if He had finally said, “Enough”?

Who could have blamed Him if He had called the angels, shattered the cross, destroyed His tormentors, and returned to the glory of heaven?

But He did not.

Even the darkness could not bend our Savior’s will.

Even abandonment could not defeat His purpose.

Even the terror of standing alone beneath the judgment for every human sin could not cause Him to turn away.

Jesus looked straight ahead into all eternity.

He saw the people His sacrifice would save.

He saw sinners forgiven.

He saw the guilty made clean.

He saw the lost brought home.

He saw broken lives restored.

He saw men, women, and children from every tribe, language, people, and nation standing before His throne.

He saw you.

And Jesus decided:

“You are worth it.”

Not because you were innocent.

Not because you had earned His mercy.

Not because you deserved the price He was paying.

You were worth it because He chose to love you with a love greater than your sin, stronger than death, and more determined than all the powers of hell.

So He remained upon the cross.

He did not call the angels.

He did not turn away.

He did not quit.

He endured the nails.

He endured the darkness.

He endured the loneliness.

He endured the full judgment for sin.

And when the price had been completely paid, Jesus declared:

“It is finished!”

What a Savior. What a King. What a Hero we have in Jesus Christ!

The Resurrection Changes Everything

If Jesus had remained in the grave, there would be no Gospel.

But on the third day, He rose bodily from the dead.

The resurrection was God’s declaration that Christ’s sacrifice was accepted, death had been defeated, and the reign of sin would not have the final word.

Jesus Christ is not merely a figure from history whose teachings survived Him. He is alive. He reigns. He saves.

Because He lives, forgiveness is real.

Because He lives, reconciliation with God is possible.

Because He lives, death is not the end for those who belong to Him.

Because He lives, hope is not wishful thinking.

It rests upon an empty tomb.

The Gospel Is News Before It Is Advice

Religion often begins with instruction:

Do this. Avoid that. Improve yourself. Perform the ceremony. Follow the rules. Prove your worth. Perhaps then you will be accepted.

The Gospel begins with an announcement:

Christ has come.

Christ has died.

Christ has risen.

The work of salvation has been accomplished.

This does not mean that the Gospel makes no demands. It calls every person to repent and believe. But repentance and faith are not payments offered to God. They do not purchase salvation.

They are the empty hands that receive what Christ has already provided.

“For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God.”

Ephesians 2:8, NKJV

Salvation is not a reward for the respectable.

It is grace for the guilty.

More Than Escaping Judgment

The Gospel is sometimes presented only as a way to avoid hell. Deliverance from judgment is certainly part of its promise, but the Gospel offers far more than escape.

Through Jesus Christ, sinners are forgiven and reconciled to God.

Those who trust Him are adopted into God’s family. They receive the Holy Spirit. Their lives are given a new direction. They begin learning to love what God loves, reject what once ruled them, forgive as they have been forgiven, and live as citizens of Christ’s kingdom.

This transformation is not completed instantly. Christians still struggle, stumble, repent, and grow. But they no longer face life alone, and they no longer belong to the darkness from which they were rescued.

The Gospel restores what sin destroyed.

Peace with God.

A cleansed conscience.

A new identity.

A living hope.

A future resurrection.

Eternal life in the presence of Christ.

The Gospel Demands an Answer

News of this magnitude cannot remain merely interesting.

Jesus did not come simply to provide inspiration, improve society, or add a spiritual dimension to otherwise unchanged lives. He came to seek and save the lost.

The Gospel confronts every person with a decision.

Will we continue trusting ourselves, defending our sin, and living apart from the God who made us?

Or will we turn to Jesus Christ in repentance and faith?

The Gospel does not ask us to pretend that we are better than we are.

It asks us to admit that we cannot save ourselves.

It does not promise that religious effort will make us worthy.

It declares that Christ is worthy and that His grace is sufficient.

It does not tell us to cross the distance to God.

It announces that God has crossed the distance to us.

That is why the Gospel is good news.

Humanity’s separation from God is real. Our attempts to repair it are powerless. But God Himself has come near in Jesus Christ.

He died for sinners.

He rose from the dead.

He offers forgiveness, reconciliation, new life, and eternal hope to all who repent and believe.

The distance has been crossed.

The way home is open.

And His name is Jesus Christ.

Do not let this good news pass you by. If you are ready to turn to Jesus Christ, ask Solomon 2.0, “How can I be saved?”

Wisdom. Truth. Hope in Jesus Christ.
